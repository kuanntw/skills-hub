# Investment review framework

## 1. Transaction and cap table

Verify:

- Current issued shares.
- Fully diluted shares.
- Founder ownership.
- ESOP/options/warrants.
- Preferred shares and liquidation preferences.
- Convertibles and SAFE-like instruments.
- Existing investors.
- Proposed primary versus secondary shares.
- Proposed price per share.
- Pre-money and post-money valuation.
- Post-round ownership.

Red flags:

- Paid-in capital presented as valuation.
- Ownership percentage inconsistent with investment amount.
- Different sections use different valuations.
- Missing dilution from options/convertibles.
- Secondary sale described as company financing.

## 2. Historical financial quality

Review at least three periods when available:

- Revenue and growth.
- Gross margin.
- Operating expense by major category.
- Operating income.
- Net income after tax.
- Operating cash flow.
- Cash balance.
- Debt and contingent liabilities.
- Accounts receivable aging.
- Customer concentration.
- Revenue recognition quality.

Separate audited/filing data from management accounts.

## 3. Revenue quality

Classify revenue:

- Recurring subscription/license.
- Usage-based.
- Project/implementation.
- Hardware resale.
- Consulting/service.
- Government grant/subsidy.
- One-time related-party or exceptional item.

Do not apply SaaS/ARR multiples to non-recurring revenue without adjustment.

## 4. Valuation support

Build a valuation table with at least:

- Implied transaction valuation.
- Historical or last-round reference.
- Comparable multiple method when usable.
- Scenario valuation based on next 12-24 month operating outcomes.

For each method show inputs, source, calculation, result, and limitation.

## 5. Forecast credibility

Create a driver tree rather than accepting top-line CAGR.

Examples:

B2B SaaS:
`customers x seats/customer x price x retention`

Services/project business:
`qualified opportunities x win rate x average contract value x delivery capacity`

Marketplace:
`active users x transaction frequency x GMV per transaction x take rate`

Training/assessment:
`enterprise accounts x participants/account x price/participant + platform/license fees`

Check whether hiring and delivery capacity can support forecast revenue.

## 6. Use of funds

For each item, connect amount to:

`resource -> deliverable -> KPI -> commercial result -> financial result`

Flag capital that merely covers structural losses without a credible inflection milestone.

## 7. Unit economics and runway

When data is available calculate:

- Gross margin.
- CAC.
- Payback period.
- LTV or gross-profit LTV.
- Net revenue retention / retention.
- Contribution margin.
- Monthly burn.
- Runway.
- Breakeven month.
- Capital needed to next milestone.

## 8. Technology and IP

Check:

- Core IP ownership.
- Founder/employee/contractor assignment.
- Dependency on third-party model/API/provider.
- Data rights and training-data provenance when material.
- Defensibility from workflow, proprietary data, distribution, integration, or domain assets.
- Cost-to-serve and inference economics for AI products.

Do not treat use of a frontier model as proprietary technology.

## 9. Market and competition

Prefer bottom-up market sizing tied to reachable buyers and pricing.

Review:

- ICP.
- Buying owner and budget.
- Procurement cycle.
- Existing substitutes.
- Direct competitors.
- Switching cost.
- Distribution advantage.
- Evidence of willingness to pay.

## 10. Team and governance

Review:

- Founder commitment.
- Relevant operating track record.
- Key-person concentration.
- Finance leadership.
- Sales leadership.
- Board/governance maturity.
- Succession and hiring gaps.

## 11. Exit and listing readiness

Assess potential paths:

- Strategic acquisition.
- Secondary transaction.
- Buyback where structurally feasible.
- Taiwan IPO/TWSE/TPEx path.
- Other capital-market route if applicable.

For a listing-readiness review, identify gating items, target evidence, responsible owner, and sequence.

## 12. Scenario analysis

At minimum include base and downside if the proposal depends heavily on forecasts.

Useful sensitivities:

- Revenue growth.
- Gross margin.
- Hiring pace.
- Sales-cycle delay.
- Customer churn.
- Model/infrastructure cost.
- Next-round valuation.
- Next-round dilution.

Avoid false precision. Use ranges when assumptions are weak.
